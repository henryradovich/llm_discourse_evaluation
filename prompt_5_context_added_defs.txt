A discourse mode is the primary communicative function

Discourse modes describe HOW a speaker communicates

DEFINITIONS:

1. Argumentation: 
    If the segment presents a viewpoint, judgment, interpretation, accusation, defense, recommendation, or evaluative framing about a topic. Its purpose is to lead the listener or reader toward a conclusion.
    A segment can be argumentative even if it does not use obvious opinion words like “I think,” “bad,” or “wrong.” Argumentation can also be created through selective facts, loaded labels, causal explanations, contrasts, jokes, sarcasm, or repeated examples that all point toward the same judgment.
    Basically, it is Argumentation when it is trying to make the audience believe, support, oppose, blame, praise, condemn, or reinterpret something.

2. Narration: 
    If the segment introduces one or more particular events, actions, or happenings  into the discourse. These events are specific rather than general, and they can usually be placed on an explicit or implied timeline.
    Narration does not always require a long story. A single specific event can count as Narration if it reports something that happened. When there is more than one event, the events are usually temporally related: one happens before, after, during, or as a result of another.
    Basically, it is Narration when the segment tells what happened, what happened next, or what someone did or said at a particular time.

3. Description: 
    If the segment presents the qualities, features, appearance, setting, atmosphere, or sensory details of a person, place, object, or scene. Its purpose is to help the listener or reader picture what something is like.
    Description is usually more static than Narration. It does not mainly move time forward. Instead, it gives a snapshot, image, scene, or impression.
    Description can include vivid or mildly evaluative words like “beautiful,” “rugged,” “dark,” “crowded,” or “massive.” But if the details are mainly selected to make the audience judge, blame, mock, or condemn someone, then it becomes Argumentation.
    Basically, it is Description when the segment helps the audience picture something. It is Argumentation when the description is mainly pushing the audience to judge something.

4. Exposition: 
    Default rule: If the segment does not clearly fit Argumentation, Narration, Description, or Emotion Expression, then tag it as Exposition.
    If the segment gives neutral explanation or background information. This includes definitions, clarifications, "how this works" explanations, general truths, factual context, procedural information, and habitual or recurring patterns.
    Exposition should be used when the segment is not mainly trying to persuade, not reporting a particular event on a timeline, not mainly creating a visual/sensory picture, and not mainly expressing an internal feeling.
    Basically, it is Exposition when the segment is helping the audience understand something in a neutral or informational way.
    Exposition also covers phatic speech, that is, language which establishes, maintains, and signals social relationships. This type of language is common in casual conversation. 

5. Ad Read: 
    If the segment is part of an identifiable advertisement, sponsorship message, paid promotion, or host-read promotional placement in a podcast, tag it as Ad Read. This label identifies the segment's promotional function and context, rather than the particular rhetorical strategy used in its wording.
    An Ad Read can include product descriptions, factual information, personal stories, persuasive claims, discount codes, calls to action, or brief sponsor acknowledgments. The sponsor does not need to be named in every sentence of the placement. When a segment belongs to a clearly identified ad break, keep Ad Read as the primary label even when an individual sentence would otherwise be Narration, Description, Exposition, or Argumentation.
    Basically, it is Ad Read when the speaker is presenting a recognizable promotional message to listeners, rather than simply discussing a product, person, show, or service in ordinary conversation.
    Important distinction: Advertising is not the same kind of category as Argumentation, Narration, Description, or Exposition. It is a context/function label.

6. Unclear: 
    If the segment does not contain enough interpretable information to reliably determine its primary discourse mode, tag it as Unclear. This category is intended for incomplete, unintelligible, severely mistranscribed, or contextually ambiguous speech that cannot be confidently assigned to Argumentation, Narration, Description, Exposition, or Ad Read.
    Unclear should be used only when the communicative function cannot reasonably be determined, even after considering the surrounding conversation.
    A segment should not be marked Unclear simply because it contains multiple discourse modes, informal language, profanity, humor, or grammatical errors.
    Basically, it is Unclear when the annotator cannot reliably determine what the speaker is communicating or what rhetorical function the utterance serves.
    Select Unclear only when the intended communicative function cannot be recovered after examining context.
    Do not select Unclear merely because multiple labels initially appear plausible.
